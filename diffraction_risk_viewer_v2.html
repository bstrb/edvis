Scientific conventions and visibility

Direct cell A has columns a, b, c in a right-handed Cartesian crystal frame. B = A⁻ᵀ; g = B(h,k,l) in Å⁻¹, with no 2π. The incident beam k₀ points along fixed laboratory +Z with |k₀| = 1/λ. At zero tilt its crystal direction is A[uvw] normalized. Lab X is the projection of a perpendicular to that beam (fallback b); Y = Z × X. Crystal rotations apply about fixed lab X then fixed lab Y. The detector axes stay fixed during these tilts.

Exact radial excitation error: s_h = |k₀ + Bh| − |k₀|. The implementation uses its algebraically equivalent rationalized form to avoid cancellation. The Gaussian uses exp[−(s/s₀)²], with no factor ½. Its sign is immaterial. All distances in the environmental sum are full 3D reciprocal distances.

Normally displayed targets are nonzero integer hkl satisfying lower d* ≤ |g| ≤ upper d*, |s_g| ≤ s_display, and k₀,Z + g_Z > 0 (forward projection). Projection axes are detector slopes X = g_X/(1/λ+g_Z), Y = g_Y/(1/λ+g_Z), dimensionless. Neither display filters nor pan/zoom change any target's score. The selected hkl is scored even outside these filters; its optional dashed projection is not a normally displayed spot.

For every target, all nonzero integer offsets Δhkl with |BΔhkl| ≤ r_cut are enumerated; h = g + Δhkl can be outside the display sphere or excitation window. There is no zone slab, index cap, structure factor, zone-axis bonus, or intensity model. The direct beam has s₀₀₀ = 0 and E(000) = 1; when eligible it contributes exp[−(|g|/σ_C)²], regardless of reference-marker visibility. A relative numerical cutoff tolerance of 64 machine epsilons × max(1, r_cut) Å⁻¹ includes boundary neighbors.

Units: cell lengths, wavelength and d spacings in Å; angles in degrees; reciprocal lengths, excitation errors, s₀, σ_C and r_cut in Å⁻¹. hkl and [uvw] coefficients are dimensionless. Gaussian weights, environmental sums and active scores are dimensionless. Defaults are exploratory, not a calibrated prediction of multiple scattering.
